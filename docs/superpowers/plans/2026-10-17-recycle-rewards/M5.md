  **Why each step-2 result is fixed.**
  - In "checkout then ship-lock step 2", `raceWithHold` starts `lock()` only after checkout waits on the held row, and releases only after `lock()` also waits on it (`lockWaiters >= 2`). Step 1 has already committed by then. So checkout reads `shipLockedAt` under its row lock and aborts with the paused 409. Then step 2 cancels the request.
  - In "ship-lock step 2 then checkout", step 2 is first in the row's lock queue. Checkout's `FOR UPDATE … status = 'OPEN'` re-evaluates after the cancel commits and finds no row, so it fails with the gone 409. The paused 409 is also accepted, in case Postgres grants the waiters in the other order.

  **Wait condition.** Every contender of the four race cases really queues on the held row: `lock()` step 1 runs the flags `createMany` (an INSERT with ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING, still taking the FK `KEY SHARE` on `users`, which conflicts with the held `FOR UPDATE`), step 2 updates the request row, and checkout takes the request row `FOR UPDATE` and later inserts the order (FK `KEY SHARE` on `users`). For step 2, `first` reaches the held row only after step 1 and the notify, inside the default `waitUntil` budget of `raceWithHold` (200 polls of 25 ms). A timeout means a contender short-circuited before the lock: report it, do not add a sleep.
